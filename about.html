About This Journal

An independent educational journal about Yellow Jambhala, the wealth deity of Tibetan Buddhism, and the thangka paintings made in his honour.

About This Journal

Yellow Jambhala Thangka Journal is an independent educational journal about one deity and the paintings made in his honour. It is not a shop, not a gallery and not a representative of any seller. It exists to help readers understand what a Yellow Jambhala thangka shows, why it is painted the way it is, and how it has been used by practitioners across the Himalayas.

The journal was written for readers who have seen one of these golden paintings — in a monastery, a shop, a museum or a friend's home — and wanted to know more. Who is the pot-bellied golden figure? Why does he hold a mongoose? What is in the cup? Why is he yellow? Each article answers one of those questions in depth, with the symbols explained in the order a teacher would point to them.

How We Work

Everything in this journal is based on the painting itself and on the descriptions that Himalayan artists and teachers pass on. We describe only what can be seen in the paintings and what the tradition says about them: the mongoose that showers jewels, the skull cup of nectar, the lineage to the yaksha king Kubera, the guardianship of the northern direction, and the craft of mineral colour and gold on cotton canvas. We do not invent histories, awards or testimonials, and we make no promises about wealth or fortune.

What We Cover

  • The meaning of Yellow Jambhala and his symbols
  • The history from Kubera, the yaksha king, to the Buddhist guardian
  • Wealth and generosity in Buddhist practice
  • Choosing, hanging and caring for a thangka at home
