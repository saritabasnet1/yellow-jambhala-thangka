An independent journal about the golden wealth deity of Tibetan Buddhism — his mongoose, his skull cup, his history, and the thangkas painted in his honour.

The Wealth Deity You Can Read

Yellow Jambhala is the best-known wealth deity in Tibetan Buddhism, a golden pot-bellied figure seated in ease. His right hand holds a jewel-spitting mongoose, said to shower the gems that relieve poverty, while his left cradles a skull cup of nectar, signs of the generosity that attracts prosperity. This journal is an independent educational guide to that deity and to the paintings made in his honour.

The paintings are handmade on cotton canvas, in luminous yellow mineral pigment with gold brocade robes, edged in brocade and ready to hang. Lineages trace the deity to the yaksha king Kubera, reframed in Buddhism as guardian of the northern direction and protector of practitioners. This journal follows that story, symbol by symbol, so that a viewer can learn to read a Yellow Jambhala thangka the way a reader reads a page.

What You Will Find Here

Five long articles take you from the meaning of the deity, through the mongoose and the skull cup, through the history from Kubera to Jambhala, through wealth and generosity in practice, and finally to the practical work of choosing, hanging and caring for a painting at home. Each article explains its terms as it goes, and each one links to the others, so you can follow a single subject in depth or move across subjects and compare them.

How Should You Use This Journal?

Read slowly. A thangka rewards the same patience as a long text. Look at one detail at a time — the mongoose, the cup, the yellow skin, the gold robes — and then step back and see how the details fit together. The articles in this journal follow that order: first the whole figure, then its parts, then the teaching that holds them together.
